Results

Most of my case studies did not survive.

I went back through 39 client accounts and checked every number against the raw account data instead of the reports. Seven survived. Those are below, with what each one does not prove.

Why so few?

Because most marketing numbers fall apart when you open the account. A lead gets counted as revenue. A best month gets quoted as the average. A close rate that exists nowhere except the case study using it turns leads into dollars.

None of that is lying, exactly. It is what happens when nobody checks. So I checked, threw out the ones that did not hold, and kept the caveats attached to the ones that did.

If a number below looks smaller than what you see elsewhere, that is the reason.

Tracked revenue 55 months lifetime, 24 months captured

Home services · plumbing + HVAC

The account that could only see its own revenue for one of three eras.

Three agency eras on one account. Only one of them could see the revenue.

What this one does not prove
  • Booked revenue is channel-mixed across roughly ten sources. Most of it is not paid search, and none of it is credited to the ad account here.
  • 64% of revenue arrives unattributed, so every per-channel figure is a floor rather than a measurement.
  • No improvement is claimed. The eras differ in what each could measure as well as how each performed, and the data cannot separate the two.
  • The eras are not like-for-like: one is entirely Search, another roughly a quarter Performance Max.
  • No change log exists for any era, so nothing attributes a result to a specific action.
Modelled 15 months

Regional broadband ISP

What it actually costs to acquire a subscriber, against the industry benchmark.

$246 cost per acquired subscriber on one platform-market, against a $300-500 industry SAC benchmark

What this one does not prove
  • This is modelled, not measured. Every conversion is a lead; no purchase event exists, so no revenue figure and no return can be stated.
  • The figure is one platform in one market. The same close rate gives materially higher costs in the other market and on social. Read the table, not the best number in it.
  • The close rate is the client's own, taken from their sales reporting. It is quoted, not estimated.
  • No lifetime value, payback or return is available: the client's reporting records no churn model.
  • Lead volume itself carries a caveat, because several form actions are counted at once.
Tracked revenue 3 clean contiguous months (Mar excluded as padded)

Apparel · Shopify

A platform setting was adding a fixed $74 to every new-customer order, and a second was adding $74 again to lapsed customers. The account was reporting revenue it had not earned. Both were dropped to $0.01, and the fix is checkable in the client's own exports by differencing reported value against original value.

CA$63,146 spend to CA$385,651 tracked Shopify revenue, 6.11x over three clean contiguous months

What this one does not prove
  • Three contiguous months, stated in Canadian dollars. The ratio is currency-neutral; the dollar totals are not.
  • The earlier month affected by value inflation is excluded rather than restated, so no platform-added value reaches this total.
  • No lift is claimed. The padded period is not a 'before' to improve on, because its numbers were not measuring the right thing.
  • The final month was captured on its closing day and is still settling.
Tracked revenue 13 consecutive months

Apparel / CPG · Shopify

Thirteen straight months of tracked storefront revenue.

$131,218 spend to $1,315,875 tracked Shopify revenue, 10.03x blended over 13 contiguous months

What this one does not prove
  • Thirteen contiguous months of tracked storefront revenue, blended. The 15.43x peak month is a peak and is labelled as one.
  • No improvement is claimed. There is no engagement document and no change history, so this is sustained performance under management, not a lift.
  • Nothing here speaks to incrementality. Tracked return is what the platform measured, not what the business would have lost without it.
  • The final month was captured on its closing day and is still settling, so the figure is conservative rather than optimistic.
Tracked revenue 40 days

Membership / subscription brand

A 40-day launch window, measured to the sale.

$3,427 spend to $16,627 tracked revenue, 4.85x, $19.06 per sale

What this one does not prove
  • Forty days. Short window, small absolute spend, and nothing here extrapolates to a month or a year.
  • Tracked purchase revenue, not an assigned value.
Tracked revenue 90-day audit window plus an 8-month series

Jewellery e-commerce

Small budget, real purchase tracking.

Small-spend Performance Max account with genuine tracked purchase revenue

What this one does not prove
  • Small absolute spend over a short window. A supporting data point, not a headline.
  • No intervention record exists, so no result is attributed to any action.
Tracked revenue 2026-05 to 2026-07, 3 contiguous months

Industrial tooling e-commerce

Three clean months, no padding.

$7,281 spend to $41,201 tracked revenue, 5.66x blended

What this one does not prove
  • Three contiguous months with no value padding. The most recent was captured on its closing day and is still settling, so the figure is conservative.
  • Blended is 5.66x. June peaks at 7.21x, and a peak is labelled a peak.
  • Small absolute spend. Nothing here extrapolates.
  • Revenue is brand-heavy, so the incremental contribution of paid search is not established.
  • No before-and-after and no change log, so this is sustained performance rather than a lift.
